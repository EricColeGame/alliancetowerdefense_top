## The Three Inputs That Actually Move a Unit's Price

### Rarity is the anchor, not the answer

Every value sheet starts with rarity because it's the easiest thing to sort. But rarity only describes how often a unit appears from the summon pool — it says nothing about whether anyone wants the result. A high-rarity unit that underperforms in the modes people actually play will slide down a tier list within weeks.

### Demand is the real driver

Demand is what separates a "rare" unit from an "expensive" one. Community reports consistently show that units which solve a specific, painful problem — clearing a wave type, holding a lane solo, or carrying a team through a late difficulty spike — hold value far better than units that are merely uncommon.

### Supply shifts with every update

The game's own description promises weekly updates, and each one reshuffles supply. A balance pass that buffs a forgotten unit creates instant demand with no new copies entering circulation. A nerf does the opposite overnight. That's why a value sheet you saved two weeks ago may already be misleading you.

## How to Run a Trade Through an Alliance Tower Defense Value Calculator

Don't just paste two unit names and trust the output. Run the trade as a process.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | List every item on both sides, including filler | Bulk pieces often close a gap of 5–10% |
| 2 | Pull each unit's current value from two separate sheets | Cross-checking exposes stale data |
| 3 | Check the last update date on each sheet | Values older than a patch cycle are unreliable |
| 4 | Confirm demand with recent trade chatter | A value sheet can't measure hype |
| 5 | Total both sides and calculate the gap | Aim for within roughly 10% for a clean trade |
| 6 | Ask whether you'd rebuy the unit at that price | The simplest gut check there is |

Step 6 is the one most players skip. If you wouldn't spend the equivalent coins to reacquire what you're receiving, the math on the **Alliance Tower Defense value calculator** is telling you something your instincts already knew.

## Reading the Value Meta Without Getting Burned

Values in a summon-based tower defense settle into loose tiers. Knowing which tier you're operating in prevents the classic mistake of treating every "rare" pull as a jackpot.

| Tier | Player label | Typical behavior |
|---|---|---|
| S | Meta / keep | Rarely traded, moves only in package deals |
| A | Strong / liquid | Most active trade volume lives here |
| B | Solid / situational | Good filler in larger trades |
| C | Niche | Value depends entirely on the buyer |
| D | Bulk | Priced in bundles, not individually |

Two rules keep you out of trouble. First, a unit sitting in Tier S with almost no trade volume isn't automatically worth more — illiquidity makes it hard to move. Second, a Tier B unit that everyone suddenly wants can outperform a Tier A unit nobody is asking about. Volume and demand beat the letter grade.

## Common Value Calculator Mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Using one sheet only | You inherit that sheet's blind spots | Compare at least two sources |
| Ignoring the update date | You price against pre-patch stats | Check the last-edited date first |
| Treating rarity as price | You overpay for a common-in-practice unit | Weight demand above rarity |
| Forgetting gamepass effects | x2 Luck and x2 Coins players flood supply | Factor supply growth into long-term holds |
| Overvaluing your own unit | Every trader does this | Argue the other side out loud |

That last row deserves emphasis. The single biggest source of bad trades is attachment. Your best unit feels more valuable because you pulled it, not because it is. A calculator's main job is to strip that feeling out of the equation.

## Building Your Own Value Sheet

If you trade regularly, a personal sheet beats any public list because it tracks the units you actually encounter. Keep it simple.

- **Unit name and tier** — your own classification, not someone else's.
- **Last known value and date checked** — a value without a date is a rumor.
- **Recent accepted trades** — the only real price discovery that exists.
- **Mode performance notes** — does it clear Hard or Insane reliably?
- **Supply notes** — is it still in the active summon pool?

Update it after every patch. The game's update cadence means a sheet that goes three weeks without edits is closer to fan fiction than data. Players who maintain even a rough sheet tend to negotiate from a position of confidence rather than hope.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Alliance Tower Defense value calculator?**
No. The tools players use are community-made sheets and trade sites, not developer-published price lists. That's why cross-checking matters — no single source is authoritative, and every one of them lags behind the current patch to some degree.

**How often should I recheck values?**
After every update, and at least weekly if you're actively trading. Balance changes and new summons can move a unit's standing in a single patch cycle, especially for units whose entire value rests on one specific stat.

**Can a value calculator tell me if a trade is fair?**
It can tell you whether the numbers are close. It can't measure whether the unit fits your team, whether you enjoy using it, or whether demand is about to spike. Treat the output as one input among several, not a verdict.

**Why do two calculators disagree on the same unit?**
Different sample sizes, different update schedules, and different definitions of "demand." A sheet built from trade-channel chatter will price hype higher than one built from long-run summon odds. When they disagree, check which one was edited more recently.

Do that consistently and the trade window stops being a gamble — it becomes a decision you can defend.
